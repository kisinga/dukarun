# DukaRun: Kitui market intelligence brief

**Decision date:** 20 August 2026  
**Purpose:** choose DukaRun's first customer segment, offer, and route to market in Kitui.

## Bottom line

Start in **Kitui Township** with established shops that have stock, staff, supplier purchases,
or customer credit to control. The first segments to test are:

1. wholesalers, hardware shops, motor-spares shops, and agrovets;
2. licensed wines-and-spirits retailers and alcohol distributors;
3. established general retailers and minimarts;
4. boutiques and electronics shops as a comparison group.

Do not lead with tiny kiosks. They are numerous, but DukaRun's KES 2,500 monthly entry price is
well above several basic POS offers. DukaRun must win on **reliable books, credit control, stock
control, and owner oversight**, not on being the cheapest till.

This is a working recommendation. Local willingness to pay, eTIMS expectations, onboarding
cost, and sustained daily use have not yet been proven.

Wines and spirits is a strong candidate because it combines fast sales, valuable stock, multiple
pack sizes, cash/M-Pesa reconciliation, staff oversight, and a real shrinkage problem. Target only
licensed businesses buying traceable stock. Regulation and enforcement make unlicensed or
informal outlets a poor customer base.

## What DukaRun is selling

DukaRun already connects POS, offline selling, stock, purchases, expenses, customer credit,
staff controls, and double-entry accounting. The public price is KES 2,500 per month for Lite
and KES 4,500 for Pro, with a 30-day trial.

The practical promise should be:

> Know what you sold, what stock you have, who owes you, and what you earned—even when you are
> away from the shop or the internet is down.

### Product risks to validate early

- The active product supports merchant-direct M-Pesa STK prompts and C2B payment notifications,
  but the public website still says STK is not available. Correct this message before outreach.
- Direct M-Pesa requires merchant Safaricom access, ownership verification, credentials, and
  activation. Measure how much this setup slows conversion.
- DukaRun does not currently submit invoices to eTIMS. KRA says all people in business must
  onboard and issue electronic tax invoices; non-VAT micro businesses can use free eTIMS Lite.
- DukaRun now supports workbook catalogue import. The remaining onboarding risk is cleaning the
  merchant's list, counting opening stock, and mapping cartons or crates to individual units.
- Pharmacies, salons, and electronics shops may expect specialist features such as dispensing,
  appointments, payroll, serial numbers, warranties, or repairs.
- No alcohol-specific age-warning, licence-hours control, excise-stamp check, or supplier
  authenticity workflow was found. DukaRun can preserve purchase records, but it cannot prove that
  a bottle is genuine.

These are not reasons to stop. They are possible deal-breakers that field research must measure.

## Local market evidence

Kitui County's 2023–2027 plan reports 10,000 registered retailers and 1,200 registered
wholesalers as at 2022. An older KIPPRA brief estimated 88,200 MSEs countywide in 2016. The large
difference reflects different dates, geography, formality, and definitions. Neither number is a
reliable sales target.

The best current local source is Kitui Municipality's business mapping file, published as the
2026 update. A direct review of the spreadsheet found:

- 6,807 rows, collected from 22 January to 4 February 2025;
- 6,601 rows with a usable business-activity description;
- 4,964 rows in Township ward, or 73% of the file;
- no usable permit numbers and only one email address;
- repeated names and inconsistent activity labels.

It is therefore useful for **route planning and sampling**, not as a clean list of unique, active,
licensed businesses.

businesses, and adjacent groups may overlap.

grouped. The 1,901-record priority pool below is a deduplicated union of matching rows, not a sum
of independently verified businesses.

Township after normalizing ward labels. Some are likely already counted as general retailers, so
do not add 180 to the 1,901-record core pool and call the result a market size.

The map is useful for finding prospects. A valid current liquor licence is the first qualification
question.

### Wines and spirits: opportunity with a regulatory gate

The commercial fit is strong: bottle-level shrinkage, purchases, cashier shifts, cash/M-Pesa
reconciliation, and owner visibility are problems DukaRun can address. The 2026 Kitui map also
shows enough density for a focused route.

The risk is unusually high. Alcohol sales require a licence and must follow its conditions. Kitui
publishes a county Act and amendment, while NACADA's 2025 national policy calls for off-licence
wines-and-spirits licensing to be prohibited. I did not find that policy enacted in the official
law sources reviewed, so its immediate legal effect should not be assumed. Confirm new-licence and
renewal practice directly with Kitui County before making this the main vertical.

Enforcement is not theoretical: an April 2026 operation in Kitui Town reportedly seized 1,843
bottles of suspected counterfeit alcohol. Purchase and supplier records may become part of the
value proposition, but DukaRun must not claim to authenticate stock or guarantee compliance.

### Target customer profile to test

- owner-managed shop with two to five staff;
- roughly 200–3,000 active products;
- at least 30 sales on a normal day;
- regular supplier purchasing or meaningful customer credit;
- owner cannot personally watch every sale;
- recent stock, cash, or debt errors worth more than the monthly subscription;
- an Android phone or computer available at the counter.

For wines and spirits, also require a current licence, stock from identifiable suppliers, at least
two people handling sales or stock, and a need to reconcile bottles, cash, and M-Pesa. Give higher
priority to operators that buy by carton or crate and sell by bottle, because the control problem
is clearer—but test that workflow before promising it.

The numbers above are screening hypotheses. Fieldwork must find the real thresholds.

## Competitive reality

The main competitor is the current workflow: notebook, calculator, M-Pesa messages, free eTIMS
Lite, and occasional help from an accountant. It costs little, is familiar, and does not require
catalogue setup.

Advertised software offers also put pressure on DukaRun:

| Offer      |                         Advertised price | Main threat                                                                        |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------: | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kiosk.ke   |            Free; paid from KES 300/month | Native M-Pesa STK, offline selling, inventory, storefront, and low price           |
| StockPoint |        KES 450 starter; KES 1,800 growth | Offline POS, M-Pesa, stock, and owner reports                                      |
| Loyverse   |            Core POS and basic stock free | Familiar global free alternative; advanced inventory is paid                       |
| DukaPlus   | KES 80,000 once; eTIMS add-on KES 30,000 | Deeper, high-touch retail system with accounting and automated M-Pesa              |
| DukaRun    |            KES 2,500 Lite; KES 4,500 Pro | Direct M-Pesa plus stronger books, credit, and controls; cannot win on price alone |

These are vendor claims from their public pages. They have not been independently mystery-shopped.

## Field intelligence plan

### 1. Interview 40 owners

Use the municipal map to select businesses systematically rather than interviewing friends:

- 12 established general retailers and minimarts;
- 10 wholesalers, hardware, and motor-spares shops;
- 8 licensed wines-and-spirits shops or alcohol distributors;
- 6 agrovets;
- 4 boutiques/electronics shops as a comparison group.

Complete 26 interviews in Township and 14 across Nzambani, Matinyani, Kyangwithya, and Mulango.
Interview the owner or purchasing decision-maker, not only the cashier.

Ask for recent facts:

- Walk me through yesterday from opening stock to closing cash.
- When did stock, cash, or customer debt last fail to balance? What did it cost?
- How many products, daily sales, staff, suppliers, and credit customers do you manage?
- What do you use today? What have you tried and stopped using?
- How do you handle M-Pesa, eTIMS, purchases, expenses, and owner reporting?
- What would make you refuse to use DukaRun?

For wines and spirits, verify the current licence and ask:

- Where does stock loss happen: receiving, shift changes, breakage, free stock, or unrecorded sales?
- Do you buy by carton or crate and sell by bottle? How are deposits and returnables handled?
- How do you confirm suppliers and products are legitimate, and which records do inspectors ask
  for?
- Do the licence conditions or permitted selling hours affect the system you need?

Test skill by asking the person to record a sale, receive stock, find a customer balance, and close
a day in DukaRun. In a wines-and-spirits shop, include receiving a carton and selling one bottle.
Do not ask people to rate their own digital skill.

### 2. Mystery-shop five alternatives

Test the status quo, Kiosk.ke, StockPoint, Loyverse, and DukaPlus against the same tasks. Measure
setup time, offline behaviour, credit, purchasing, M-Pesa, eTIMS, reports, data export, support
response, and full first-year cost. Marketing pages alone are not enough.

### 3. Run 12 assisted pilots

Recruit four businesses from each of the three leading segments: wholesalers/hardware/spares,
licensed wines and spirits, and established general retail. Set up their fastest-moving products,
opening stock, staff, and customer balances. Observe four weeks of real use.

Do not use stated willingness to pay as the result. At the end of the trial, ask for payment at
the public Lite price. If testing another price, clearly label it as a research offer and apply the
same offer consistently.

### Decision gates

Choose a segment only if the pilots show:

- at least 8 of 12 businesses record 80% or more of normal sales by week two;
- at least 6 of 12 pay KES 2,500 after the trial;
- at least 8 remain active at day 60;
- the owner can name a measured saving, recovered debt, prevented loss, or time reduction;
- assisted onboarding takes no more than three hours per business;
- ongoing support falls below 30 minutes per business per week.

To name one segment the winner, at least three of its four pilots should both pay and remain active
at day 60. Otherwise, increase that segment's sample before committing the sales strategy.

If fewer than six pay, identify whether the cause is price, eTIMS, M-Pesa activation, setup
burden, licensing uncertainty, carton-to-bottle handling, weak pain, or poor usability before
changing the price.

## Route to market

1. **Founder-led shop visits and live demos.** Start in Township, where the business map is most
   concentrated.
2. **Customer referrals.** Reward a referral only after the new customer pays.
3. **Trusted partners.** Test bookkeepers, accountants, wholesalers, and distributors that already
   advise shop owners. For wines and spirits, approach legitimate beverage distributors rather
   than using consumer-facing alcohol promotions.
4. **WhatsApp.** Use a short demo and click-to-WhatsApp ads only after one message converts in
   person.
5. **Events.** Use [KASTIE 2026](https://kitui.go.ke/county-events/kitui-agricultural-show-trade-investment-expo-kastie2026/)
   at Ithookwe Stadium on 24–26 September to recruit qualified demos, not merely collect contacts.
6. **Radio last.** A local station can create awareness, but use a unique phone number or keyword
   and stop if it does not produce activated trials.

Track qualified demos, activated trials, paid conversion, acquisition cost, founder/support time,
60-day retention, and referrals. Do not optimize for raw leads or app registrations.
